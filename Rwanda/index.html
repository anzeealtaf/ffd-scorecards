⚠️

Ambition-vs-Buffers Moment: Rwanda combines strong growth (7.2%), sound institutions and a well-capitalised banking system with rising debt and a wide external gap. Financing of the New Kigali International Airport (NKIA) & RwandAir is set to push public debt from 75% (2025) toward ~80% of GDP by 2027, against a current-account deficit near 13% of GDP and eroding policy buffers. Debt is at moderate risk of distress.

🟢

Institutional Strength

CPI at a record 57/100 (2024, top-3 in Sub-Saharan Africa) and the region's best B-READY business-environment scores signal governance well above Rwanda's income level.

🟠

Debt & Buffer Squeeze

Public debt ~75% of GDP and rising toward ~80% by 2027 on NKIA airport financing, with a current-account deficit near 13% of GDP. Moderate risk of debt distress.

🟢

Sound but Shallow Banks

NPLs just 2.6% and capital adequacy ~22% — a resilient, deposit-funded system. But financial markets stay shallow (thin FX interbank market, constrained SME credit).

🟡

Revenue & Data Gaps

Tax/GDP at 14.2%; new compensatory & comprehensive tax packages aim to add ~1pp by FY29/30. Several social-spending & market-depth indicators remain unfilled data gaps.

Primary source: IMF, Rwanda: 2025 Article IV Consultation and Sixth Review Under the PCI, Country Report No. 25/319 (Nov 14, 2025). Supplementary indicators drawn from publicly available international databases: World Bank WDI & WGI, World Bank Business Ready (B-READY 2024), Transparency International CPI 2024, PEFA Secretariat, WHO GHED, S&P/Fitch/Moody's, and the National Bank of Rwanda. Scores (1–5) reflect assessment against fixed international benchmarks for the FY2024/25 reference period.

Methodology & caveats: 24 of 25 indicators carry a directly-sourced score; the one exception is the INFF (qualitative status, shown as a narrative card). The IMF Financial Development Index was retired from the framework (data stale post-2020), giving 25 indicators. The external-debt-stocks indicator (Pillar V) was corrected in this build — it had been mislabeled "average maturity (years)" with an un-inverted scoring formula; it now reads External Debt Stocks (% of GNI, 91.3% for Rwanda — high, though concessional-heavy) with bands running the intuitive direction (lower = better), consistent with the corrected master framework. Pillar and overall averages exclude the unscored INFF. The overall score (3.6) is Provisional because PEFA is a qualitative read pending indicator-level conversion. Two Pillar-V ratios (private credit/GDP, deposits/loans) are computed from IMF monetary-survey tables and labelled as such.

Intended use: A structured conversation starter for donor–government dialogue on FfD priorities — not an official government document or a cross-country ranking. Verify against primary sources before use in formal assessments.
